Rosenmund Reduction

The Rosenmund reaction is the selective reduction of an acyl chloride to an aldehyde using hydrogen gas over palladium on barium sulfate. It prevents over-reduction to alcohol.

Catalyst: Pd/BaSO₄

Reaction: Acid Chloride → Aldehyde

Hofmann Rearrangement

The Hofmann rearrangement converts a primary amide into a primary amine with one less carbon atom through rearrangement.

Reagents: Br₂ + NaOH

Reaction: Amide → Amine

Aldol Condensation

Aldol condensation forms β-hydroxy aldehydes/ketones which dehydrate into α,β-unsaturated compounds.

Catalyst: NaOH

Reaction: Ketone → Unsaturated compound

Reimer-Tiemann Reaction

This reaction introduces a formyl group (-CHO) into phenol to produce salicylaldehyde.

Reaction: Phenol → Salicylaldehyde

Friedel-Crafts Acylation

Friedel-Crafts acylation introduces an acyl group into aromatic rings using a Lewis acid catalyst.

Catalyst: AlCl₃

Reaction: Benzene → Ketone

Finkelstein Reaction

The Finkelstein reaction is an SN2 substitution converting alkyl halides into alkyl iodides.

Reagents: NaI / Acetone

Reaction: Alkyl halide → Alkyl iodide

Cannizzaro Reaction

The Cannizzaro reaction is a redox reaction where one aldehyde is oxidized and another reduced.

Reagents: Strong base

Reaction: Aldehyde → Alcohol + Acid

Sandmeyer Reaction

The Sandmeyer reaction replaces diazonium groups with halides or cyanide using copper salts.

Reagents: Cu salts

Reaction: Diazonium → Aryl halide
